Five main labels, kept flat on purpose — Priority stays almost empty by default, so if something lands there, it's genuinely urgent and important.

Filters that do the first pass automatically

  • Sender is a board member or investor, or subject mentions "board"→Priority — Action Needed, marked important
  • Subject mentions "contract," "signature," or an urgent vendor deadline→Priority — Action Needed
  • Subject mentions "PTO," "expense," "RSVP," or "approve"→Routed to my queue, tagged under Work
  • Sender is a newsletter or marketing domain→Reference > Newsletters, skips inbox, marked read
  • Sender is a pre-approved personal contact→Personal, stays in inbox

Keeping it at zero — the daily sweep

First 15 minutes
Morning sweep

Priority label checked first — anything urgent and important gets handled or delegated before anything else.

Before lunch
Midday sweep

Clear anything new in Priority, process delegable items sitting in my queue, glance at Personal.

End of day
Close-out sweep

Priority should be empty. Archive what's resolved, flag what's scheduled for tomorrow.

Built as a portfolio template based on my Travel and Tourism Management training — the same structure I'd apply to a real executive's trip.

The problem

Trips booked reactively, one confirmation email at a time, spread across the inbox. It's easy to miss a tight connection, double-book a hotel night, or notice a missing document two days before departure.

The solution

One tracker per trip: flights, ground transport, hotel confirmations, time-zone conversions, per-diem notes, and a document checklist — cross-checked against the calendar before anything is booked.

The result

Trip planning becomes a five-minute check the week before departure, with one single source of truth instead of six scattered email threads.

  • Pre-trip: confirm dates against the calendar, flag any conflicts before booking
  • Booking: flights and hotels logged with confirmation numbers, cancellation windows, and cost
  • Documentation: passport, visa, and insurance status checked against departure date
  • Day-of: a single itinerary view with local time zones and ground transport times
  • Post-trip: expenses logged and reconciled against the trip budget

How we'll work

The Founder-First Ops Method — four stages, repeated in a weekly rhythm.

01
Discovery

A focused call, up to 30 minutes, to understand your pain points, current state, and initial preferences — how you like to communicate, what tools you're already using, and what "handled" looks like to you.

02
Setup

In week one, I start with the inbox and calendar audit, since that's usually where the most time is being lost. From there I build the rest of the initial systems and document the SOPs we'll run on.

03
Execution

I take on the day-to-day responsibilities and project-based work, keeping things moving without needing to be micromanaged.

04
Reporting

A short weekly or monthly check-in using the EOS Level 10 format — wins, scorecards, to-dos, rocks, and issues — so you always know where things stand.
